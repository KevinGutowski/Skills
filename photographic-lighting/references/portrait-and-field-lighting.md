# Portrait And Field Lighting

Distilled from Hunter/Biver/Fuqua, *Light: Science & Magic* and Tony Northrup's DSLR book — no verbatim quotes; full bibliography: [sources.md](sources.md).

The rules live in SKILL.md (one key first, then eyes, fill, separation, background exposure; the outdoor and indoor defaults). This file adds what the body leaves out: symptom-to-move diagnosis, the sequences, and failure checks. Complete setups with positions and settings: [worked-setups.md](worked-setups.md).

## Key Light Diagnosis

Extra lights are rescue or refinement. Fix the key first, reading the shadow side of the nose, cheek, brow, and chin.

| Symptom | Cause | Move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Face lacks contour | Key too frontal | Move the key farther to the side |
| Eye sockets dark, no catchlight | Key too high | Lower the source or bring it forward |
| Shadow side collapses, key triangle on the far cheek disappears | Key too far to the side | Bring it forward, or add fill |
| Skin texture and nose/chin shadows dominate | Source too small or too far | Enlarge it or bring it closer |
| Shape gone, face reads flat after fill | Fill too strong | Reduce fill until the shadow side is visibly darker again |

Fill tools, in the order to try them: reflector, bounce surface, flash exposure compensation on a fill flash, a second light.

## Catchlight Troubleshooting

Check the eyes before the stand diagram; they show where the source actually is.

- Dull eyes: turn or move the subject toward open sky, a window, or a bright wall.
- Brow shadow over the eyes: lower the key until both eyes catch it.
- Only one eye lit: the source is too far to the side; bring it forward.
- Still nothing: add a reflector below the face or a low-power fill flash.

## Outdoor Conditions

| Condition | What it gives | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloud cover | A very large, soft source | Little direction; shape comes from where the subject faces |
| Shade / open sky | Soft light, relaxed eyes | Blue or green color casts from sky and foliage |
| Sun behind or side-behind | Rim and hair separation, no squint | Face in shadow until filled |
| Low front sun | Warm direct light, saturated sky | Squinting |
| Noon sun | Little that helps a face | Needs diffusion, shade, a reflector, or fill |

Backlit portrait sequence:

1. Place the subject with the sun behind or side-behind.
2. Set the exposure for the background or sky if it matters to the picture.
3. Add face light with fill flash or a reflector.
4. Check catchlights and shadow density.
5. Reduce fill if the flash look becomes obvious.

## Indoor And Event Flash

On-camera direct flash is a small frontal source: usable when there is no time, otherwise harsh and flat. Beyond the bounce rule in the body:

- For moving event work, tip the bounced head slightly forward so some light still reaches the eyes.
- Set fill with flash exposure compensation; fill should not overpower ambient unless that is the style.

Failure checks:

| Failure | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Dark background behind a flashed face | Raise ambient exposure, or bounce so the flash lights the room |
| Orange/green room with a cool flashed face | Gel the flash to match, or correct the mix deliberately |
| Slow recycle | Lower flash output, raise ISO, use a closer bounce surface, change batteries, or remove diffusion that eats output |

## Practice Drills

Practice with simple objects before a paid or fragile subject:

- Orange or other textured object: hard side light against soft front light.
- Glossy phone or tablet: move light and camera until glare appears and disappears.
- Metal utensil: use a white card as the reflected source.
- Glass cup: bright-field, then dark-field edges.
- Black object on black: add a controlled rim.
- White object on white: separate edge from background and hold the highlights.

The habit to build: surface → shadow/reflection → camera/light geometry → fill/control.
