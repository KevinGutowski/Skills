# Worked Setups

*Scope: four complete setups that apply the rules in SKILL.md end to end. Read one when the user wants a concrete starting arrangement, not a principle.*

**These are worked examples, not recorded shoots.** Each is assembled from this skill's own rules (size, direction, family of angles, fill last). Distances, ratios, and camera settings are starting points to confirm with a test frame; they are not quoted from the sources and no result is claimed. Gear names are generic on purpose: check sync speed, flash modes, and menus against the actual camera.

## How to read a setup

- **Positions** use a clock face seen from above: subject at the center, camera at 6 o'clock. Height is given relative to the subject.
- **Ratios** are key-to-fill differences in stops on the subject: one stop is 2:1, two stops is 4:1. Judge them on the shadow side of the subject, not from power dials.
- **Camera settings** follow one logic. Aperture sets depth of field. With flash, aperture and flash power set the flash exposure, while shutter speed (at or below the camera's sync speed) sets how much ambient light records. ISO stays at base unless the light runs out. With continuous light on a tripod, shutter speed is free.
- **Change-ifs** name the first move, in the body's order: size, direction, reflection, then fill.

## Setup 1: Indoor headshot, one key and a reflector

- **Subject and surface:** one person, head and shoulders; skin is a textured, mostly matte surface.
- **Intent:** flattering, with visible facial shape and live eyes.
- **Key:** a large soft source (softbox, shoot-through umbrella, or a window with a sheer curtain) at about 4–5 o'clock, close to the subject (roughly an arm's length or two beyond the frame edge), its center a little above eye level and angled down.
- **Fill:** a white reflector at about 8–9 o'clock, close in. Start with the shadow side about one stop under the key (2:1) for a soft, open look; pull the reflector back toward two stops (4:1) for more shape.
- **Rim/background:** none to start. Add a hair or background light only if the head merges with the background.
- **Modifier logic:** the source is large and close, so it is soft; moving it back makes it effectively smaller and harder.
- **Camera rationale:** a short telephoto keeps facial proportions calm. A middle aperture (around f/4–f/5.6) holds both eyes in focus at headshot distance. Base ISO. With flash, set the shutter at or just below sync speed to keep room light out; with window light, raise ISO before letting the shutter drop into subject-motion blur.

| If | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Face looks flat | Move the key toward 3 o'clock before touching fill |
| Eye sockets dark, no catchlights | Lower the key or bring it forward |
| Skin texture too strong | Bring the key closer or use a larger source |
| Shadow side blocks up | Move the reflector closer; if still too dark, bring the key forward |
| Reflector side shows a color cast | The bounce surface is not neutral; swap it for white |
| Glasses show the source | Raise the key or turn the head slightly: the source is inside the family of angles of the lenses |

## Setup 2: Backlit outdoor portrait with fill flash

- **Subject and surface:** one person, half-length, low sun.
- **Intent:** hair and shoulder separation from the sun, a sky that keeps its color, a face that looks naturally lit.
- **Rim (the sun):** behind the subject at about 11 or 1 o'clock, so it rims the hair without shining into the lens. The sun is the rim light; nothing is added for separation.
- **Key on the face (flash or reflector):** an off-camera flash through a small softbox or umbrella at about 4–5 o'clock, slightly above eye level; or a reflector at the same position catching the sun. On-camera fill flash is the fallback when nothing else is available.
- **Ratio:** set the background first, then bring the face up until it sits about one stop under the background for a natural look, or level with it for a more lit look. Starting flash exposure compensation: about −1 stop, then adjust by eye.
- **Camera rationale:** expose for the background first (body rule): choose the aperture for depth of field, then the shutter for the sky. If that shutter is faster than sync speed, either stop down or lower ISO to get back under it, use a neutral-density filter to keep a wide aperture, or switch to high-speed sync and accept reduced flash output.

| If | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Flash look is obvious | Reduce fill by a further ⅓–⅔ stop, or enlarge/move the flash closer so it is softer |
| Face still dark at full power | Move the flash closer, remove diffusion, or open the aperture and recover the sky with shutter or ND |
| Lens flare washes the frame | Shift the subject to block the sun, or shade the lens |
| Face looks cool against a warm rim | Warm the flash with a gel to match the low sun |
| No flash available | Reflector below and in front of the face; or turn the subject to open sky and accept a brighter background |
| Subject squints | The sun has drifted toward the front; rotate the subject until it is behind again |

## Setup 3: Polished steel knife, bright metal with a gradient

- **Subject and surface:** a polished flat blade lying on a matte tabletop; the blade is a mirror, the handle is matte.
- **Intent:** bright, clean metal with a gentle gradient along the blade, no hot spots, readable handle texture.
- **Camera first:** lock the camera on a tripod, looking down at the blade at an angle. The family of angles depends on this position; set it before any light.
- **Key (the reflected surface):** find the family of angles with the mirror test, then fill it with a white card or diffusion panel larger than the zone the blade sees. For a top-down-angled camera this panel sits above and beyond the blade, around 12 o'clock, tilted toward it.
- **Light on the panel:** aim one light at the panel, not at the knife. Feather it so one end of the panel is brighter; the blade shows that falloff as its gradient.
- **Fill:** a small white card near the camera side to lift the handle, kept outside the blade's family of angles so it does not add a second reflection.
- **Rim/edge:** a narrow black card just outside the family of angles gives the blade a dark defining edge. No ratio applies to the blade itself: its brightness is the brightness of the panel it reflects.
- **Camera rationale:** tripod and continuous light make shutter speed free, so use base ISO and a smaller aperture (around f/8–f/11) to hold the whole blade in focus. Expose for the brightest part of the blade without clipping it.

| If | Change |
| --- | --- |
| A dark band crosses the blade | The panel does not cover the whole family of angles; enlarge it or move it closer |
| A hot spot appears | A bare source is visible to the blade; put it behind the diffusion or move it out of the family of angles |
| Blade is evenly white with no form | Feather the light more to steepen the gradient, or add the black edge card |
| Handle is too dark | Bring the fill card closer; do not raise the panel light |
| The blade should be dark instead | Take the panel out of the family of angles and let it reflect black; light the handle and edges separately |
| The object is curved (a spoon, a watch case) | Expect many families of angles: use a much larger panel or a tent and control everything it can see |

## Setup 4: Clear glass of liquid, bright-field

- **Subject and surface:** a clear glass with a pale drink on a white or translucent surface.
- **Intent:** a clean bright background with crisp dark edges that draw the glass; the liquid glows.
- **Key (the background):** a bright background directly behind the glass at 12 o'clock: a lit white wall, or a diffusion panel lit from behind. Size it to just fill the frame as the camera sees it. A background much larger than the frame wraps light around the glass and washes out the edges.
- **Edges:** black cards at about 3 and 9 o'clock, just outside the frame, give the edges something dark to reflect. Move them in to thicken the edge lines.
- **Fill:** none on the glass front. Front light adds reflections without defining the shape.
- **Rim:** none; in bright-field the dark edge does the rim's job.
- **Second subject:** if a label or garnish must read, add a small soft light from the front at about 5 o'clock after the edges work, placed so the glass front does not reflect it toward the camera.
- **Camera rationale:** tripod, base ISO, and a middle-to-small aperture (around f/8) so both rims of the glass are sharp. Set exposure so the background reads white while the edges stay dark; pushing the background far beyond white causes flare that eats the edges, so check the edges at full magnification.

| If | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Edges are weak or gray | Narrow the bright background to the frame, or bring the black cards closer |
| Glass disappears into the background | Same fix: more edge contrast, not front light |
| Reflections of the room on the front | Flag them, or darken the room in front of the glass |
| Liquid looks dull | Put a small white card behind the glass, cut to its shape, to return light through the liquid |
| The brief wants glowing edges on black | Switch to dark-field: dark background filling the frame, light placed at the edge-defining limits |
| White base merges with the background | Let the base fall slightly darker than the background so the foot of the glass keeps an edge |

## Adapting a setup

- Change one variable at a time and re-check from the camera position; reflections and shadow edges are only correct from there.
- Keep the order: size, direction, reflection, then fill and control.
- A setup that worked for one surface does not transfer to another. Reclassify the surface first, then pick the nearest setup as a starting point.
- When an accepted real shoot exists, record it with the promotion form in [coverage-gaps.md](coverage-gaps.md); these examples do not replace recorded results.
